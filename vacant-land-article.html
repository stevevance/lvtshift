2How many vacant parcels there are

Cook County’s classification system assesses vacant land (class 1-00) at 10% of market value, the same assessment level as homes. Commercial and industrial property is assessed at 25%. In tax year 2026 there are 58,865 class 1-00 parcels. The count fell by about a third between 1999 and 2012 and has stayed near 60,000 since.

Two other classes are also vacant land assessed at 10%: 1-90 (minor improvement on vacant land, 2,423 parcels in 2026) and 2-41 (vacant land under common ownership with an adjacent home, 25,050). Counting them, about 86,000 parcels are vacant land at the 10% level. The article uses the 1-00 count alone. Vacant lots owned by governments are usually exempt and are not in these counts.

4What happens if vacant land is assessed at 25%

The model raises the assessment level on class 1-00 vacant land from 10% to 25%, the commercial level and the maximum Cook County’s classification rules allow. Nothing else changes. In the revenue-neutral version, each taxing body collects the same total as in tax year 2024, so its rate is recalculated over the larger tax base. Vacant land pays $183 million more, and every other kind of property pays a little less; about 64% of that relief goes to residential property.

This is not a land value tax. Nothing here shifts tax off buildings and onto land, and there is no split rate or building abatement. The model changes one thing only, the assessment level on vacant land, and leaves the rest of the property tax exactly as it is today.

Categories use the Assessor’s standard groupings, except that the reassessed vacant lots get their own “Vacant land (reassessed)” row. Every other row is not reassessed: under “revenue-neutral” it sees a small decrease; under “not revenue-neutral” it is unchanged. Positive means the parcel pays more.

Exactly which parcels are reassessed. Only the x-00 vacant-land classes currently assessed below the 25% commercial level: overwhelmingly class 1-00 “Vacant Land” (about 59,000 parcels), plus a few dozen 2-00/3-00/8-00 vacant codes. Those parcels, and only those, make up the “Vacant land (reassessed)” row. Two related classes are vacant land but are left untouched, and are broken out as their own rows so nothing is hidden: class 2-41 (“vacant land under common ownership with an adjacent residence,” homeowner side lots), and the handful of 5-00 parcels already assessed at the 25% commercial level. The reform only ever raises toward the target and never lowers.
Why class 1-90 is excluded. The Assessor also has class 1-90, “Minor Improvement on Vacant Land” (about 2,600 parcels), which it files alongside vacant land. A sample across the county shows it is too mixed to reassess as one class: some are genuinely empty lots that arguably belong in 1-00; some are surface parking lots that belong in the commercial 5-90 class; and some are truly undevelopable, like land under high-tension power lines or beneath the CTA “L” tracks, that correctly stays 1-90. Rather than raise the assessment on all of them, we exclude 1-90 and flag it for parcel-by-parcel reclassification.

Model limitation: current tax uses gross equalized value (homeowner and senior exemptions and TIF are not removed), so dollar levels run about 8% above the official $19.2 billion extension. The revenue-neutral distribution is unaffected. Levies and rates: Cook County Clerk, tax year 2024 tax code agency rate file; each of about 900 taxing bodies (TIF districts excluded) recalculates its own rate.

5What happens when a vacant lot gets built on

Of the 60,361 parcels classed 1-00 in tax year 2023, 1,767 had a building by tax year 2024 (they moved to a residential, apartment or commercial class). Their combined assessed value rose from $25.0 million to $248.3 million (+893%), and their combined tax bills rose 882%, from $6.2 million to $60.4 million: about $54 million more billed in a single year.

New revenue or relief? For taxing bodies under the state’s tax cap (PTELL), new construction raises the levy they may collect, so it is new revenue. For bodies that set their levy regardless of the tax base, the new value instead lowers everyone else’s rate a little. Cook County has both kinds.

Fewer vacant lots get built on than before 2008

In 2008 the Cook County Board passed Ordinance 08-O-51, which lowered the assessment level for vacant land from 22% to 10% starting in tax year 2009. Before the change, about 3,000 vacant lots a year got a building. Since 2014 it has been about 1,000 to 1,300 a year. The change took effect during the recession and financial crisis, so the drop can’t be blamed on it alone, but a lower assessment level hasn’t brought construction back.

6Why this isn’t a split-rate land value tax

A split-rate land value tax, used by Harrisburg, Allentown and other Pennsylvania cities, taxes land at a higher rate than buildings. Cook County’s classification system is different: it assesses property at different levels depending on its use, and every parcel in a tax code pays the same rate.

The Illinois Constitution (Art. IX, §4) lets Cook County classify property by use, as long as the highest assessment level is no more than 2.5 times the lowest. It does not provide for taxing land and buildings differently, so a split-rate land value tax would need action in Springfield. Raising vacant land from 10% to 25% fits within the existing limit and needs only a vote of the Cook County Board of Commissioners.
